WhatsApp for your desktop, written in Rust. Native on Windows, macOS and Linux, with no browser inside.

A real app, not a tab.

Zeus links to your phone the way WhatsApp Web does, then draws every chat itself. Replies, reactions, voice notes, files and link previews live in one quick window that opens in a blink.

Bright by day.

Light mode is paper white and quiet, made for a sunny desk.

Dark by night.

When the light goes, Zeus follows your system and turns over in one stroke: wallpaper, bubbles and all.

Click anywhere in the storm to call down a bolt.

Twenty themes, light and dark.

Dracula, Nord, Tokyo Night and seventeen more, each in both modes. Or mix your own look, mode and accent. Try a few on the window.

Calls that carry.

Answer when the group rings and see everyone at once, each on their own tile, with your camera beside them.

Echo cancelling keeps your speakers out of the call and lifts quiet voices.

Turn on push to talk, then hold Space when the room is loud.

How linking works.

To begin, open Zeus. It shows a QR code, or a code to type if you would rather link with your phone number.

On your phone, open WhatsApp, go to Linked devices and choose Link a device. Your phone stays the main account, just as it does for WhatsApp Web.

Once linked, your recent chats arrive. Scroll up in any chat and Zeus asks your phone for older history.

From then on, your messages live in an encrypted archive on this computer, its key held by your system's keyring. Nothing goes to a server of ours, and an app lock keeps the window private when you step away.

The Windows installer is not code-signed, so SmartScreen warns once: choose More info, then Run anyway. The Mac app is not signed by Apple either, so the first time, right-click Zeus in Applications and choose Open. The macOS and Linux builds come from GitHub Actions; please report what you find.
